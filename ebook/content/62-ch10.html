Your First Ninety Days

One model, one niche, one offer, ten conversations, and a weekly habit of getting better.
Learning objectives
  • Follow a ninety-day plan from decision to paying customers
  • Set up the business basics every model needs
  • Run a weekly review that catches each model’s trap early
  • Stack models over time into a business that’s hard to copy

Choosing a model is the beginning, not the work. This chapter gives you a common ninety-day structure that works for every model, the business setup you’ll need regardless of which one you choose, a weekly review habit, and a map of how the eight models stack into something bigger over time.

A note on taxes and legal setup

Keep business and personal money separate from day one, set aside a portion of revenue for taxes, and save every receipt. Rules on business formation, sales tax, and self-employment tax vary by location, so an hour with a local accountant early on is one of the best investments you can make.

The Weekly Review

Block thirty minutes at the same time every week, and answer five questions in a simple tracker:

  • Customers: How many conversations, sign-ups, and paying users this week?
  • Revenue: What came in?
  • Learning: What surprised me? What did customers ask for?
  • Trap check: Am I falling into my model’s trap? (See Table 10.3.)
  • One priority for next week.

Stacking Models Over Time

The strongest businesses rarely stay in one model. They start with one that earns money quickly, then add models that are harder to copy.

Chapter summary
  • Ninety days is enough to decide, validate, build a small version, serve first users, and start charging.
  • Every model needs the same business basics: a legal entity, separate banking, simple bookkeeping, payments, and an owned audience.
  • A thirty-minute weekly review, including a trap check, keeps you honest.
  • Stack models over time, from fast-earning models toward ones that compound and are hard to copy.
